## The creative, as it actually runs

**Primary text (verbatim):**

> The part nobody puts on the job description: spending hours writing up everything you do into a document so that someone else can follow it. 😣
>
> It doesn't have to be that way.
>
> With Scribe, you do the task. It captures everything you do, clicks and keystrokes, and builds a guide automatically, breaking down every step.
>
> You do your job. The documentation takes care of itself.
>
> Get started at scribe.com

**Video:** a real creator talking straight to camera, filmed selfie-style, handheld, in an everyday setting (a car), with large TikTok-style captions burned in. It opens on the POV line "POV you wasted too much time not knowing this exists," runs a short spoken monologue about the hours lost writing things down for someone else, and lands on a playful "👉 scribe.com 👈" end card. CTA button: **Learn more.** Length about 40 seconds.

Scribe runs the same documentation-pain message across **more than one UGC execution.** Alongside the car monologue, a second cut is a two-person desk skit ("you gotta use Scribe... use the Guide Me") that cuts to a real screen recording of the product building a guide, then the same scribe.com card. Same message, different creator, different room. That reuse is exactly what Stage 1 rewarded, and it is a signal in itself: Scribe keeps re-shooting this one idea because it works.

## Hook

The first two seconds do two jobs at once:

- **Names a pain you feel but never see written down.** "The part nobody puts on the job description" (text) and "you wasted too much time" (video) both point at the invisible tax of documenting your own work. It is oddly specific, which is what makes it feel true.
- **Opens a curiosity gap.** "not knowing this exists" promises a fix without naming it, so you keep watching to find out what "this" is.

No logo, no product, no brand voice in the hook. It earns the watch first, then introduces Scribe.

## Angle

**Documentation is a hidden, resented chore, and the product removes it entirely.** The promise is not "make better docs faster," it is "you stop doing this at all": *you do your job, the documentation takes care of itself.* The value proposition is subtraction, not a feature. That reframe (the work you hate simply disappears) is the emotional core.

This is precisely Guidde's space. Guidde captures a workflow and turns it into a step-by-step video guide, so the same "the documentation writes itself" promise is available to Guidde nearly word for word, only with **video** as the output instead of a written doc.

## Structure

A clean four-beat arc, the standard high-performing UGC shape:

1. **POV hook** (0-3s): relatable pain + curiosity gap, creator to camera.
2. **Agitate** (3-15s): the specific misery, hours spent writing up what you do so someone else can follow it, delivered as lived experience, not a bullet list.
3. **Reveal + magic** (15-35s): the tool, and the "it builds itself" moment. In the demo cut this is a real screen recording of capture → auto-generated guide; in the monologue cut it is described.
4. **CTA** (35-40s): a low-friction, playful end card (scribe.com) and a soft "Learn more."

## Visual style

- **Authentic over produced.** Handheld, selfie framing, natural light, an ordinary location. It reads as a person sharing a tip, not a brand running an ad, which is why it survives in-feed next to organic content.
- **Legible with sound off.** Big burned-in captions carry the whole message muted, essential for Facebook/Instagram autoplay.
- **The product appears as relief, not a tour.** When the screen recording shows up, it is the payoff to the tension, on screen only long enough to land the "it builds itself" beat.
- **Playful, human CTA.** The 👉 emoji end card keeps the casual creator register all the way to the click.
